\section{The Born rule is equivalent to the von Neumann entropy}

\paragraph{Files.}\leavevmode
\begin{itemize}

\item Library: \lean{BornRuleEntropy}
\item Imports: \lean{ForMathlib.Analysis.Convex.AffineOnBall},

  \lean{ForPhyslib.QuantumMechanics.Qubit.BlochBall},
  \lean{ForPhyslib.QuantumMechanics.VonNeumannEntropyMixture}
\item New \lean{ForMathlib} material:
  \lean{ForMathlib/Analysis/Convex/AffineOnBall.lean}: a real function on a real normed space that
  is affine along every chord of the unit sphere is affine on the closed unit ball
  (\lean{exists_linearMap_eq_of_chord}); in an inner product space, if moreover $f \le 1$ on the
  sphere, $f(a) = 1$ and $f(-a) = 0$ for a unit vector $a$, then
  $f(v) = (1 + \langle a, v\rangle)/2$ (\lean{eq_of_chord_of_le_one}).
\item New \lean{ForPhyslib} material:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{ForPhyslib/QuantumMechanics/VonNeumannEntropyMixture.lean}: a mixture
  $\sum_i p_i\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_i}$ of orthonormal pure states, in any dimension, is a density
  operator (\lean{isDensityOperator_tsum_smul_proj}) with entropy $\sum_i \eta(p_i)$,
  $\eta(x) = -x\log x$ (\lean{vonNeumannEntropy_tsum_smul_proj}); the entropy of an equal mixture
  of two pure states is $h((1 + |\braket{\psi|\phi}|)/2)$, $h$ the binary entropy
  (\lean{vonNeumannEntropy_mix_proj}); every density operator is such a mixture, of countably many
  orthonormal pure states, its eigenvectors with nonzero eigenvalue
  (\lean{IsDensityOperator.exists_tsum_smul_proj}).
\item \lean{ForPhyslib/QuantumMechanics/Qubit/BlochBall.lean}: the Bloch ball of the plane spanned by
  orthonormal $\psi, \chi$ in $E$. The point $r \in \mathbb R^3$ is the operator
  $\tfrac12(\ket\psi\bra\psi + \ket\chi\bra\chi + r\cdot\sigma)$
  (\lean{blochOperator}), affine in $r$; unit Bloch vectors are the pure states of the plane; the
  Born probability of two of them is $(1 + a\cdot b)/2$, so orthogonal means antipodal; and every
  point of the ball is the mixture of the ends of a diameter through it.
\item \lean{ForPhyslib/QuantumMechanics/Qubit/Bloch.lean}: every unit vector of $\mathbb R^3$ is
  the Bloch vector of a unit vector of $\mathbb C^2$ (\lean{exists_blochVector_eq}).
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Setting.} $E$ is a complex Hilbert space of any dimension. A pure state is
$\ket\psi\bra\psi$ (\lean{proj}~$\psi$) for a unit vector $\psi$, and an ensemble is a density
operator (\lean{IsDensityOperator}: positive, trace class, trace one). The entropy
$S$ is a function from operators to $[0, \infty]$, and the von Neumann entropy
\lean{vonNeumannEntropy} is $\operatorname{tr}\eta(\rho) \in [0, \infty]$ with the natural
logarithm, so the brief's ``$S = 1$'' (one bit) reads $S = \log 2$. The conditional probability
is a function $p$ of two operators; $p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, \rho)$ is the probability of finding
$\psi$ having prepared $\rho$. The conditions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \lean{EnsemblesAndStates}~$p$ (EQST): $p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, \rho) \in [0, 1]$ for density
  operators $\rho$; $p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, t\ket{\phi_1}\bra{\phi_1} +
  (1 - t)\ket{\phi_2}\bra{\phi_2}) = t\,p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, \ket{\phi_1}\bra{\phi_1}) +
  (1 - t)\,p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, \ket{\phi_2}\bra{\phi_2})$ for $t \in [0, 1]$; and
  $p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, \ket\phi\bra\phi) = 1$ iff $\ket\psi\bra\psi = \ket\phi\bra\phi$;
\item \lean{BornRule}~$p$ (BR-BORN): $p(\ket\psi\bra\psi, \ket\phi\bra\phi) =
  |\braket{\psi|\phi}|^2$;
\item \lean{IsVonNeumannEntropy}~$S$ (BR-ENT): $S(\rho) = $ \lean{vonNeumannEntropy}~$\rho$ for
  density operators $\rho$;
\item \lean{MutualExclusivity}~$S\ p$ ([B]MUTEX): for every countable family $(\psi_i)$ of unit
  vectors, $p(\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_i}, \ket{\psi_j}\bra{\psi_j}) = 0$ for all $i \ne j$ iff
  $S(\sum_i w_i\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_i}) = \sum_i \eta(w_i)$ for all weights $w_i \ge 0$ with
  $\sum_i w_i = 1$ (the sum of operators converging in operator norm);
\item \lean{CountablyAffine}~$p$ (the affinity of the brief's EQST): for every countable family
  $(\psi_i)$ of unit vectors and weights $w_i \ge 0$ with $\sum_i w_i = 1$,
  $\sum_i w_i\,p(\ket\phi\bra\phi, \ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_i})$ converges to
  $p(\ket\phi\bra\phi, \sum_i w_i\ket{\psi_i}\bra{\psi_i})$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Differences from the brief.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $E$ is a Hilbert space of any dimension; separability is not used. [B]MUTEX quantifies over
  countable index types (in the universe of $E$). For BR-BORN + [B]MUTEX $\Rightarrow$ BR-ENT the
  eigenbasis of $\rho$ is restricted to the eigenvectors with nonzero eigenvalue, a countable
  family since the eigenvalues are summable.
\item Entropies take values in $[0, \infty]$, not in $\mathbb{R}$: the von Neumann entropy and the
  Shannon entropy of a countable family of weights can be infinite (see ``Problems found'').
\item In \lean{EnsemblesAndStates}, affinity is assumed only for mixtures of two pure states,
  which is weaker than the brief's countable mixtures, so \lean{two_conditions_imply_third} is
  stronger than Theorem~9. The brief's countable affinity is \lean{CountablyAffine}; it implies
  the two-state affinity, and Theorem~9 over the brief's EQST follows
  (\lean{two_conditions_imply_third_of_countablyAffine}). EQST is used only for BR-ENT +
  [B]MUTEX $\Rightarrow$ BR-BORN, and there only through $p(\psi|\psi) = 1$, affinity for two
  pure states, and $p \ge 0$ on pure states (the algebraic proof) or $p \le 1$ on pure states
  (the Bloch-ball proof). The other two directions do not use EQST. Corollary~8 needs countable
  affinity: in infinite dimension an ensemble is in general only a countable mixture of pure
  states.
\item The first argument of $p$ is the projection $\ket\psi\bra\psi$, so $p$ depends on the ray of
  $\psi$ only.
\item In BR-ENT + [B]MUTEX $\Rightarrow$ BR-BORN, [B]MUTEX is used only for pairs of orthogonal
  states, and only in the direction ``Shannon entropy $\Rightarrow$ mutually exclusive''. In
  BR-BORN + [B]MUTEX $\Rightarrow$ BR-ENT only the other direction is used.
\item Independence of the conditions is not in the brief; its conclusion only says that
  [B]MUTEX is the one extra requirement. The formalization shows that none of BR-BORN, BR-ENT and
  [B]MUTEX implies another over EQST, when $E$ has two orthonormal vectors (in dimension at most
  one, EQST alone implies BR-BORN). The model for [B]MUTEX alone satisfies it in a degenerate
  way: $p(\psi|\phi) \ge \tfrac12$ for all pure states, so no two states are mutually exclusive,
  and every mixture of two or more states has entropy $0$ or $\infty$, never a Shannon entropy
  $\log 2$. In each model the failure of one of the two remaining conditions follows from
  Theorem~9.
\item BR-ENT + [B]MUTEX $\Rightarrow$ BR-BORN is proved twice. The first proof follows Fig.~3.
  The states of the plane spanned by $\psi$ and a unit $\chi \perp \psi$ form a Bloch ball, on
  which $f = p(\psi|\cdot)$ is affine along every chord, since the ends of a chord are pure states;
  $f \le 1$, $f = 1$ at $\psi$ and $f = 0$ at the antipode $\chi$. Such a function is
  $(1 + a\cdot r)/2$ with $a$ the Bloch vector of $\psi$ (\lean{eq_of_chord_of_le_one}): on a unit
  $e \perp a$ its linear part vanishes, because otherwise $f$ would exceed $1$ on the sphere near
  $a$. On the sphere $(1 + a\cdot b)/2$ is the Born rule. The second proof is algebraic, in the
  same qubit. From $f(\psi) = 1$ and $f(\chi) = 0$, the convex identities
  $\ket u\bra u + s(r - s)\ket\chi\bra\chi =
  r(r - s)\ket\psi\bra\psi + 2rs\ket{e_\zeta}\bra{e_\zeta}$
  (for $r \ge s$, and symmetrically for $r \le s$), with $u = r\psi + s\bar\zeta\chi$ and
  $e_\zeta = (\psi + \bar\zeta\chi)/\sqrt2$, give $f(u) = r^2 + 2rs(f(e_\zeta) - \tfrac12)$, and
  $\ket{e_\zeta}\bra{e_\zeta} + \ket{e_{-\zeta}}\bra{e_{-\zeta}} = \ket\psi\bra\psi +
  \ket\chi\bra\chi$ gives $f(e_\zeta) + f(e_{-\zeta}) = 1$. If $d = f(e_\zeta) - \tfrac12 < 0$,
  the state with $r/s = -d$ has $f(u) = -d^2/(1 + d^2) < 0$, so $f(e_\zeta) = \tfrac12$ and
  $f(u) = r^2 = |\braket{\psi|u}|^2$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Problems found in the brief.} None that affect Theorem~9; some statements need care.
\begin{itemize}
\item EQST takes $S : \mathcal E(\mathcal H) \to \mathbb R$, but in infinite dimension the von
  Neumann entropy is $+\infty$ for some density operators, and $-\sum_i p_i\log p_i$ can be
  $+\infty$ for countably many weights. With a real-valued $S$, BR-ENT and [B]MUTEX cannot hold in
  infinite dimension, and Theorem~9 is true there only vacuously. The formalization uses
  $[0, \infty]$.
\item The units are not fixed: eq.~(2) writes $\log$, while Corollary~7 and Fig.~2 use entropy
  $1$ for two orthogonal states, which needs $\log_2$.
\item BR-ENT calls $-\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log\rho)$ ``the Shannon entropy''.
\item The proof of BR-ENT + [B]MUTEX $\Rightarrow$ BR-BORN refers to ``the proof of 1'' and
  ``the same trigonometric arguments in the proof of 1''; there is no item~1, and
  Proposition~3 and Corollary~7 are meant. It also says ``$p$ must take its extreme values on
  $\psi$ and $\phi$'', where $\psi$ and $\psi^\perp$ are meant.
\item In the same proof, ``Given that the range of $p$ is $[0,1]$, $p$ must take its extreme
  values on $\psi$ and $\psi^\perp$. Therefore, $\psi$ and $\psi^\perp$ must be opposite points on
  the ball'' does not say why the affine function $p(\psi|\cdot)$ has its gradient along the axis
  through $\psi$ and $\psi^\perp$; that two orthogonal states are antipodal already holds in
  the Bloch ball of EQST. The missing step is that an affine function on the ball takes its
  maximum only in the direction of its gradient, so $p \le 1$ with $p(\psi|\psi) = 1$ puts the
  gradient along $\psi$ (\lean{eq_of_chord_of_le_one}). The brief also does not say why
  $p(\psi|\cdot)$ is affine on the whole ball when EQST only makes it affine in mixtures of pure
  states: every segment of the ball lies on a chord, whose ends are pure states
  (\lean{exists_linearMap_eq_of_chord}). With these two steps the argument of Fig.~3 is
  formalized as stated (``Differences'', last item).
\end{itemize}
