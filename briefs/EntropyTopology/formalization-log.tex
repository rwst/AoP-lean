\paragraph{Setting.} A measure space $(X, \mu)$ (\lean{MeasureTheory.Measure}). The predicate
\lean{IsDensity} says that $\rho \colon X \to \mathbb{R}$ is measurable, pointwise
nonnegative, integrable with $\int \rho\, d\mu = 1$, and that $\rho \log \rho$ is integrable.
This is the space $P_\mu$; as a type it is \lean{Density}. The entropy $S$ and the
Jensen--Shannon divergence are in bits, and $d_1(\rho, \sigma) = \int |\rho - \sigma|\, d\mu$.
The probability measure $p_\rho$ with density $\rho$ is \lean{prob} $\mu\,\rho$, and the total
variation distance is Mathlib's \lean{tvDist}, i.e.\ $|p_\rho - p_\sigma|(X)$.

For a divergence $D$ there are two topologies ``generated by the balls''
$\{\sigma : D(\sigma, \rho) < \delta\}$. In \lean{divTopology} $D$ a set is open iff it contains
a ball around each of its points; \lean{ballTopology} $D$ is generated by declaring all balls
open. For a metric they agree; in general the second is finer. With $D = \mathrm{JSD}$ they are
\lean{jsdTopology} and \lean{jsdBallTopology}; \lean{tvTopology} is \lean{divTopology} of
$\mathrm{tvDist}(p_\sigma, p_\rho)$. The $L^1(\mu)$ topology \lean{l1Topology} is induced by
$\rho \mapsto [\rho] \in L^1(\mu)$ (\lean{Density.toL1}).

Two results go beyond the brief and answer the questions it leaves open. A measure \emph{has a
disjoint sequence} (\lean{Measure.HasDisjointSeq}, ForMathlib) if there are pairwise disjoint
measurable sets $A_n$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$, with $0 < \mu(A_n) < \infty$. A topology on $P_\mu$
is \emph{admissible} (\lean{Admissible}) if mixing
$[0, 1] \times P_\mu \times P_\mu \to P_\mu$ (with the product topology) and $S$ are continuous.
The \emph{entropy topology} \lean{entropyTopology} is induced by
$\rho \mapsto ([\rho], S(\rho)) \in L^1(\mu) \times \mathbb{R}$ (\lean{entropyEmbedding}), i.e.\
by the pseudometric $\max(d_1(\rho, \sigma), |S(\rho) - S(\sigma)|)$; the type synonym
\lean{EntropyDensity} carries this pseudometric. The skew Jensen--Shannon divergence
$\mathrm{JS}_\lambda(\rho, \sigma) = S(\lambda\rho + (1-\lambda)\sigma) - \lambda S(\rho)
- (1-\lambda) S(\sigma)$ is \lean{skewJsd}, written as one integral.

\paragraph{Differences from the brief.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Densities are functions, not $L^1$ classes, and are required to be measurable and
  nonnegative everywhere rather than almost everywhere. ``$S(\rho) \in \mathbb{R}$'' is read as
  integrability of $\rho\log\rho$.
\item Lemma~4 is proved through the binary entropy. With $p = (1+u)/2$ it says
  $(2p-1)^2/2 \le \ln 2 - H(p) \le \ln 2\,|2p-1|$. The lower bound comes from concavity of
  $H(p) + 2(p - \tfrac12)^2$, using $H'' = -1/(p(1-p)) \le -4$, which is the brief's
  second-derivative argument. The upper bound comes from concavity of $H$, the brief's chord
  argument.
\item Eq.~(18) uses Cauchy--Schwarz. The formal proof uses the pointwise inequality
  $|a-b| \le \frac{(a-b)^2}{(a+b)\,2c} + \frac{c(a+b)}{2}$ with $c = d_1/2$, which gives the same
  bound $d_1^2 \le 2\int (\rho-\sigma)^2/(\rho+\sigma)$.
\item Eq.~(12) is formalized through convergence along filters. The topological statements are
  formalized twice. First through neighbourhoods (\lean{eventually_*}), then as equalities and
  inequalities of topologies on \lean{Density}. Since JSD is not a metric, ``the topology
  generated by the open balls'' is formalized in both readings (see the setting). Theorem~46
  then reads $\tau \le$ \lean{jsdBallTopology}, the stronger of the two, which the brief's
  argument gives directly: continuity of $\sigma \mapsto \mathrm{JSD}(\sigma, \rho)$ makes each
  ball $\tau$-open. Densities are functions, so these topologies do not separate densities that
  agree almost everywhere; they are not $T_0$.
\item Eq.~(2) is formalized in both readings of $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ (see the problem
  below). The norm reading is proved for any two integrable densities by a Hahn decomposition
  $\{\rho \le \sigma\}$; no $\sigma$-finiteness of $\mu$ is needed, which matters since
  Theorem~21 uses infinite $\mu$.
\item In Theorem~21 the hypothesis $1 < \mu(A_n)$ is dropped. Only $\mu(A_n) \to \infty$ is
  used, and all statements hold eventually in $n$. The first remark (counting measure) is not
  formalized separately; it is the special case $X$ countable.
\item Theorem~46 is proved in two forms. The main form assumes only that the \emph{equal}
  mixture $(\rho, \sigma) \mapsto (\rho+\sigma)/2$ is continuous. That is weaker than the
  brief's hypothesis that all mixing is continuous, so the formal statement is stronger. The
  \lean{_of_mixture} forms take the brief's hypothesis literally, reading ``statistical
  mixing (e.g.\ $(\lambda, \rho, \sigma) \mapsto \lambda\rho + (1-\lambda)\sigma$)'' as this
  map $[0, 1] \times P_\mu \times P_\mu \to P_\mu$ (\lean{Density.mixture}), with the product
  topology. They follow by fixing $\lambda = \tfrac12$.
\item The brief asks for ``the appropriate'' topology on $P_\mu$; which properties make a
  topology appropriate is a modelling choice and is not formalized. The mathematical part is:
  admissible topologies exist, there is a coarsest one, and it is Hausdorff on $L^1$ classes.
  Its pseudometric uses $\max$; the sum $d_1 + |\Delta S|$ gives the same topology.
\item The characterization of $L^1$-continuity is stated with disjoint sequences rather than
  with dimension. A disjoint sequence exists iff $L^1(\mu)$ is infinite-dimensional (the
  indicators $1_{A_n}$ are linearly independent; conversely, without a disjoint sequence the
  finite part of $\mu$ consists of finitely many atoms). This equivalence is not formalized.
\item Literature on discontinuity. Ho and Yeung (``On the discontinuity of the Shannon
  information measures'', IEEE Trans.\ Inf.\ Theory 55 (2009) 5362, Theorem~2) show that for
  every distribution $P^0$ of finite entropy on a countably infinite alphabet, including finitely
  supported ones, and every $c \in (0, \infty]$, there are $P_n$ with
  $\chi^2(P^0 \,\|\, P_n) \to 0$ and $H(P_n) \to H(P^0) + c$. This is the counting-measure case of
  \lean{not_continuousAt_entropy}, in a stronger mode of convergence and with any prescribed
  jump. Counting measure has spreading but no concentration, and discrete entropy is lower
  semicontinuous (their Sec.~I), so only upward jumps occur there. Wehrl (Rev.\ Mod.\ Phys.\ 50
  (1978) 221), cited there, treats the quantum analogue: von Neumann entropy is discontinuous in
  infinite dimension. Harremoës (``Information topologies with applications'', in
  \emph{Entropy, Search, Complexity}, Springer 2007, Theorem~21) shows that for discrete
  distributions under convergence in information divergence, which is finer than $L^1$, the
  entropy is continuous at power-dominated distributions and discontinuous at hyperbolic ones;
  so finer topologies do have continuity points. Piera and Parada (arXiv:0710.1275) give
  sufficient conditions, such as bounded densities, for convergence of differential entropy; the
  continuous case here is of this kind, with a uniform bound.
\item Literature on the entropy topology. For discrete distributions it already appears in Ho
  and Yeung (Sec.~VI). With $\Psi(P, Q) = H(\tfrac12 P + \tfrac12 Q) - \sqrt{H(P) H(Q)}$,
  $\sqrt\Psi$ is a metric (their Theorem~10), and $\Psi(P_n, Q) \to 0$ iff $H(P_n) \to H(Q)$ and
  $d_1(P_n, Q) \to 0$ (their Theorem~9). Both topologies are first countable, so on a countable
  alphabet the topology of $\sqrt\Psi$ is the entropy topology. They also use
  $D(Q \,\|\, P) + |H(Q) - H(P)|$. Their motivation is typicality, not mixing, and $\Psi$ needs
  $H \ge 0$, so it does not extend to densities. The pseudometric
  $\max(d_1, |\Delta S|)$ works for every $\mu$. We found no prior characterization of the
  entropy topology as the coarsest topology making mixing and entropy continuous
  (\lean{isGreatest_entropyTopology}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Problems found in the brief.} No mathematical errors. One notational ambiguity and
one understatement:
\begin{itemize}
\item Eq.~(2), $\|p_\rho - p_\sigma\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = \tfrac12\|\rho - \sigma\|_{L^1(\mu)}$, does
  not say which total variation is meant. With the usual total variation norm
  $|p_\rho - p_\sigma|(X)$, which is Mathlib's \lean{tvDist}, the factor $\tfrac12$ is wrong:
  $|p_\rho - p_\sigma|(X) = \|\rho - \sigma\|_{L^1(\mu)}$ (\lean{tvDist_prob}). The factor is
  right for the distance $\sup_A |p_\rho(A) - p_\sigma(A)|$ (\lean{isGreatest_abs_prob_sub}).
  Nothing downstream depends on the constant, since only convergence is used.
\item The conclusion extends the failure of $L^1$-continuity to ``typical infinite-dimensional
  spaces''. In fact it fails at every point of every $P_\mu$ for which $\mu$ has a
  disjoint sequence, i.e.\ $L^1(\mu)$ is infinite-dimensional (\lean{not_continuousAt_entropy}),
  and holds otherwise
  (\lean{continuous_entropy_iff}). Theorem~21 covers only spreading. Lebesgue measure on
  $[0, 1]$, where every set has finite measure, is a concentration case: mixing in a little of
  the uniform density on a short interval sends $S$ to $-\infty$.
\end{itemize}
Gaps the formalization had to fill:
\begin{itemize}
\item Prop.~10 and Theorem~46 use $(\rho+\sigma)/2 \in P_\mu$, i.e.\ that the mixture has finite
  entropy. The brief does not prove this. It follows from the pointwise bounds
  $0 \le \mathrm{JSD}\text{-integrand} \le \tfrac{\ln 2}{2}|\rho-\sigma|$
  (\lean{isDensity_mid}).
\item Theorem~46 presupposes that $P_\mu$ is closed under mixing, which the brief does not
  prove either. For $m = \lambda a + (1-\lambda) b$ and $\eta(x) = -x\ln x$, concavity and
  subadditivity of $\eta$ give
  $0 \le \eta(m) - \lambda\eta(a) - (1-\lambda)\eta(b) \le \eta(\lambda)\,a + \eta(1-\lambda)\,b$,
  so $\eta(m)$ is integrable (\lean{isDensity_mixture}).
\item The last sentence of Prop.~10 needs more than eq.~(11) in the second reading. The bounds
  make each JSD ball an $L^1$ neighbourhood of its centre, which gives
  \lean{jsdTopology_eq}. For the topology generated by the balls, each ball must be $L^1$-open,
  i.e.\ $\sigma \mapsto \mathrm{JSD}(\sigma, \rho)$ must be $L^1$-upper semicontinuous; the
  brief does not address this. The formal proof shows full continuity. For
  $\sigma_n \to \sigma$ in $L^1$, a subsequence converges a.e.\ (via convergence in measure).
  The integrand $g_n$ is split into $\min(g_n, G)$, with $G = \tfrac{\ln 2}{2}(\sigma + \rho)$,
  which converges by dominated convergence, and the excess $g_n - \min(g_n, G)$, which is at
  most $\tfrac{\ln 2}{2}|\sigma_n - \sigma|$ by eq.~(17). This is the generalized dominated
  convergence theorem, with dominating functions $\tfrac{\ln 2}{2}(\sigma_n + \rho)$ converging
  in $L^1$ (\lean{MeasureTheory.tendsto_integral_of_dominated_of_tendsto_L1}, ForMathlib;
  \lean{tendsto_integral_jsdDensity}).
\item In Theorem~21, $\varepsilon_n = 1/\log_2\mu(A_n) \in (0, 1)$ needs $\mu(A_n) > 2$, not
  $\mu(A_n) > 1$. The brief's ``passing to a tail'' covers this.
\end{itemize}
