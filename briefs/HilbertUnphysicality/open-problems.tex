\noindent
Open problems raised by the technical brief in this directory, and by its Lean formalization
(see \lean{formalization-log.tex}).
Items marked \textsc{[blocks verification]} stand in the way of checking a main
claim of the brief as the brief states it: a \emph{formalization gap} can be closed
in Lean alone, while a \emph{brief issue} needs the brief to be corrected or made
precise first.

\section*{From the brief}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Further problems.} The list of ways in which QSTO:H fails the base conditions is
  open-ended: ``If the reader is aware of other problems that should be added, do contact us.''
  (Brief, Sec.~1, footnote~1.)
\item \textbf{A physically adequate model.} The brief concludes that ``the solution is not to
  find workarounds for these issues, but to fix the mathematical model to better reflect the
  physical requirements''. A replacement for QSTO:H that satisfies [B]COBS, [B]WDQ and
  [B]STDIST is not given here; one candidate is the brief ``Physical vector spaces for quantum
  mechanics''. (Brief, Sec.~5.)
\item \textbf{Expectations of a general unbounded observable.} [B]COBS is about the expectation
  $\psi \mapsto \braket{\psi|O|\psi}$, while ``boundedness is equivalent to continuity'' is about
  everywhere-defined linear maps; an unbounded observable $O$ is only densely defined. That the
  expectation of every unbounded observable is discontinuous is not proved. The formalization
  covers everywhere-defined linear maps (\lean{not_continuous_of_unbounded}) and the oscillator
  example for $N$ only. (Brief, Sec.~3, proof of Theorem~1, first paragraph; Log,
  ``Results''.)
\end{enumerate}

\section*{From the formalization}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \blocker{brief issue}
  \textbf{The conditions themselves.}
  [B]COBS, [B]WDQ, [B]STDIST and the inconsistency
  claimed in Theorem~1 are informal; only the mathematics behind each of the three arguments is
  checked. Precise readings are proposed in the last section; once they are confirmed, each
  part of Theorem~1 becomes a theorem about QSTO:H that can be checked in Lean.
  (Log, ``Setting''.)
\item \textbf{The unitary between the two frames.} The unitary $U$ that implements the frame
  change $y(x) = \tan\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{erf}(x)\bigr)$ on wave functions, and the
  change of variables in the integral, are not formalized; only the densities $|\psi|^2$,
  $|\phi|^2$ and eq.~(2) are. So is the general claim that ``we can find a unitary $U$ that
  connects operators with different domains''. (Brief, Sec.~3; Log, ``Differences''.)
\item \textbf{Point-wise values.} That $\psi(x)$, and so $|\psi(x)|^2$, is not defined at each
  point of a ray of $L^2$ is not formalized. (Brief, Sec.~4; Log, ``Differences'', last item.)
\item \textbf{Commutator relations.} That $[X,P]/(i\hbar) = I$ cannot hold on the whole of
  $\mathcal{H}$ and must be restricted to a domain is not formalized. (Brief, Sec.~4; Log,
  ``Differences'', last item.)
\item \textbf{Domain of the Schr\"odinger equation.} That the Schr\"odinger equation holds
  only on the domain of $H$, since $P\psi$ and $V(x)\psi(x)$ need not be defined, is not
  formalized. (Brief, Sec.~4; Log, ``Differences'', last item.)
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Proposed precise readings}

The following readings are proposed to resolve the first item of the previous section. They are
chosen so that the arguments of the brief go through as written, and they need to be confirmed
against the intent of the brief.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} $\mathcal{H}$ is infinite-dimensional. QSTO:H allows any separable
Hilbert space, but in finite dimension every self-adjoint operator is bounded and defined
everywhere, so [B]COBS and [B]WDQ hold and Theorem~1 is false as stated.

\paragraph{Inconsistency.} ``QSTO:H is inconsistent with C'' means: for every separable,
infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$, the model that QSTO:H builds on
$\mathcal{H}$ fails condition C. For [B]STDIST, $\mathcal{H}$ is replaced by the family of
state spaces of different systems.

\paragraph{[B]COBS.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{States} are the nonzero vectors of $\mathcal{H}$, with the norm topology; this is
  equivalent to the quotient topology on $P(\mathcal{H})$, since everything below is invariant
  under $\psi \mapsto c\psi$.
\item An \emph{observable} is a self-adjoint operator $O$ with domain $\mathcal{D}(O)$.
\item The \emph{expectation} of $O$ is
  $\langle O\rangle_\psi = \braket{\psi|O\psi}/\braket{\psi|\psi}$, defined for
  $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(O)\setminus\{0\}$.
\item [B]COBS: for every observable $O$, $\psi \mapsto \langle O\rangle_\psi$ is continuous
  on $\mathcal{D}(O)\setminus\{0\}$ with the subspace topology.
\item Claim to be proved: if $O$ is densely defined, symmetric and unbounded (for every $C$
  there is $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(O)$ with $\|O\psi\| > C\|\psi\|$), then
  $\psi \mapsto \langle O\rangle_\psi$ is discontinuous at \emph{every} point of
  $\mathcal{D}(O)\setminus\{0\}$; and every infinite-dimensional separable $\mathcal{H}$
  carries an unbounded self-adjoint operator. The oscillator example of the brief is a special
  case. This also settles the third item of ``From the brief''.
\item Alternative: define $\langle O\rangle_\psi$ on all of $\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}$
  through the spectral measure of $O$, with values in $(-\infty,+\infty]$ for $O$ bounded
  below. For the number operator this is $\sum_n n\,|c_n|^2/\sum_n |c_n|^2$, which is lower
  semicontinuous but not continuous, so [B]COBS fails under this reading too.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{[B]WDQ.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{frame} is a coordinate on the configuration space $\mathbb{R}$; a \emph{change of
  frame} is a $C^1$ bijection $y = f(x)$ of $\mathbb{R}$ with $f' > 0$.
\item A change of frame acts on states by the unitary
  $U_f\colon L^2(\mathbb{R}, dx) \to L^2(\mathbb{R}, dy)$,
  $(U_f\psi)(y) = \psi(f^{-1}(y))\,\bigl((f^{-1})'(y)\bigr)^{1/2}$, so that $|\psi|^2$
  transforms as a density (eq.~(2)).
\item The \emph{corresponding quantity} in the new frame is the quantity of the same kind in
  the new coordinate: each observer measures position with the multiplication operator by
  their own coordinate, on its maximal domain. So $X$ and $Y$ are the same operator on
  $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, multiplication by the variable, applied to $\psi$ and to $U_f\psi$.
  It is \emph{not} $U_f X U_f^{-1}$: that operator has domain $U_f\,\mathcal{D}(X)$, and with
  it [B]WDQ would hold automatically.
\item A quantity $Q$ is \emph{well defined} in a state $\psi$ if $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(Q)$.
\item [B]WDQ: for every change of frame $f$ and every state $\psi$,
  $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(X)$ implies $U_f\psi \in \mathcal{D}(X)$.
\item Claim to be proved: for $f(x) = \tan(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{erf}(x))$ and
  $\psi = (e^{-x^2}/\sqrt\pi)^{1/2}$, $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(X)$ but
  $U_f\psi = (1/(\pi(1+y^2)))^{1/2} \notin \mathcal{D}(X)$. This also settles the second
  item of ``From the formalization''.
\item Abstract form, for the sentence ``we can find a unitary $U$ that connects operators with
  different domains'': if frames may be related by any unitary $U$, [B]WDQ says
  $U\,\mathcal{D}(O) \subseteq \mathcal{D}(O)$ for every observable $O$ and every unitary $U$.
  By Hellinger--Toeplitz $\mathcal{D}(O) \ne \mathcal{H}$ for unbounded $O$, and a unitary
  that maps a unit vector of $\mathcal{D}(O)$ to one outside it violates this.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{[B]STDIST.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{systems} are those with $n \ge 1$ degrees of freedom. For $n = 0$,
  $L^2(\mathbb{R}^0) = \mathbb{C}$ is finite-dimensional and the argument does not apply.
\item The \emph{mathematical representation} of a system is its state space
  $\mathcal{H}_n = L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$, together with what QSTO:H builds from it alone:
  $P(\mathcal{H}_n)$, $D(\mathcal{H}_n)$, the self-adjoint operators, the Born rule and the
  von Neumann entropy.
\item Two representations \emph{tell systems apart} unless they are unitarily equivalent.
  Every part of the QSTO:H structure is carried along by a unitary
  $\mathcal{H}_n \to \mathcal{H}_m$, so unitary equivalence of the Hilbert spaces suffices.
\item [B]STDIST: for $n \ne m$ there is no unitary $\mathcal{H}_n \to \mathcal{H}_m$.
\item Claim to be proved: for all $n, m \ge 1$ there is one
  (\lean{L2_euclidean_iso}, already checked).
\item Caveat for the brief: if the representation also includes the positions
  $X_1, \dots, X_n$ and the momenta $P_1, \dots, P_n$, then $n$ is part of the data and
  [B]STDIST holds trivially. The argument needs the observables to be left out, and the brief
  should say so. Under the proposed reading, [B]STDIST fails even in finite dimension: the spin
  of an electron and the polarization of a photon are both represented on $\mathbb{C}^2$.
\end{itemize}
