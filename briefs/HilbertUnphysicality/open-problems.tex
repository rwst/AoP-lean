\noindent
Open problems raised by the technical brief in this directory, and by its Lean formalization
(see \lean{formalization-log.tex}).
Items marked \textsc{[blocks verification]} stand in the way of checking a main
claim of the brief as the brief states it: a \emph{formalization gap} can be closed
in Lean alone, while a \emph{brief issue} needs the brief to be corrected or made
precise first.

\section*{From the brief}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Further problems.} The list of ways in which QSTO:H fails the base conditions is
  open-ended: ``If the reader is aware of other problems that should be added, do contact us.''
  (Brief, Sec.~1, footnote~1.)
\item \textbf{A physically adequate model.} The brief concludes that ``the solution is not to
  find workarounds for these issues, but to fix the mathematical model to better reflect the
  physical requirements''. A replacement for QSTO:H that satisfies [B]COBS, [B]WDQ and
  [B]STDIST is not given here; one candidate is the brief ``Physical vector spaces for quantum
  mechanics''. (Brief, Sec.~5.)
\item \textbf{Expectations of a general unbounded observable.} [B]COBS is about the expectation
  $\psi \mapsto \braket{\psi|O|\psi}$, while ``boundedness is equivalent to continuity'' is about
  everywhere-defined linear maps; an unbounded observable $O$ is only densely defined. That the
  expectation of every unbounded observable is discontinuous is not proved. The formalization
  covers everywhere-defined linear maps (\lean{not_continuous_of_unbounded}) and the oscillator
  example for $N$ only. (Brief, Sec.~3, proof of Theorem~1, first paragraph; Log,
  ``Results''.)
\end{enumerate}

\section*{From the formalization}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \blocker{brief issue}
  \textbf{The conditions themselves.}
  [B]COBS, [B]WDQ, [B]STDIST and the inconsistency
  claimed in Theorem~1 are informal; only the mathematics behind each of the three arguments is
  checked. (Log, ``Setting''.)
\item \textbf{The unitary between the two frames.} The unitary $U$ that implements the frame
  change $y(x) = \tan\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{erf}(x)\bigr)$ on wave functions, and the
  change of variables in the integral, are not formalized; only the densities $|\psi|^2$,
  $|\phi|^2$ and eq.~(2) are. So is the general claim that ``we can find a unitary $U$ that
  connects operators with different domains''. (Brief, Sec.~3; Log, ``Differences''.)
\item \textbf{Point-wise values.} That $\psi(x)$, and so $|\psi(x)|^2$, is not defined at each
  point of a ray of $L^2$ is not formalized. (Brief, Sec.~4; Log, ``Differences'', last item.)
\item \textbf{Commutator relations.} That $[X,P]/(i\hbar) = I$ cannot hold on the whole of
  $\mathcal{H}$ and must be restricted to a domain is not formalized. (Brief, Sec.~4; Log,
  ``Differences'', last item.)
\item \textbf{Domain of the Schr\"odinger equation.} That the Schr\"odinger equation holds
  only on the domain of $H$, since $P\psi$ and $V(x)\psi(x)$ need not be defined, is not
  formalized. (Brief, Sec.~4; Log, ``Differences'', last item.)
\end{enumerate}
