\noindent
Open problems raised by the technical brief in this directory, and by its Lean formalization
(see \lean{formalization-log.tex}). Items marked \emph{research} are open research questions,
not needed to check the brief. No open problem blocks verification of the brief.

\section*{From the brief}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Further problems.} \emph{Research.} The list of ways in which QSTO:H fails the
  base conditions is open-ended: ``If the reader is aware of other problems that should be
  added, do contact us.'' (Brief, Sec.~1, footnote~1.)
\item \textbf{A physically adequate model.} \emph{Research.} The brief concludes that ``the
  solution is not to find workarounds for these issues, but to fix the mathematical model to
  better reflect the physical requirements''. A replacement for QSTO:H that satisfies [B]COBS,
  [B]WDQ and [B]STDIST is not given here; one candidate is the brief ``Physical vector spaces
  for quantum mechanics''. That candidate meets [B]WDQ only for changes of frame that map its
  space onto itself. For its Schwartz example, the change of frame of Proposition~3 is not one:
  the Gaussian $\psi$ is a Schwartz function and $U\psi$ is not
  (\lean{QuantumVectorSpaces.FurtherResults.not_forall_frameUnitary_mem_range}). A replacement
  therefore has to say which changes of frame it supports. It cannot support all smooth ones:
  no nonzero state has finite position variance in every smooth frame both at time~0 and after a
  free evolution (\lean{FurtherResults.not_forall_frames_freeEvolution}; see \lean{further-results.tex}, Sec.~2).
  Carcassi, Calder\'on and Aidala exclude the change of frame of Proposition~3 for Schwartz
  spaces (arXiv:2308.06669v3, Appendix, Sec.~VIII). The remark
  after Theorem~5 that ``Schwartz spaces present the same problem'' concerns [B]STDIST only.
  A space that does not have that problem is the Gelfand--Shilov space
  $S^{1/2}_{1/2}(\mathbb R^n)$: for different $n$ these spaces are not isomorphic as locally
  convex spaces (\lean{briefs/QuantumVectorSpaces/further-results.tex}, Sec.~5, not formalized).
  It is not a quantum vector space in the sense of Definition~1 of that brief.
  (Brief, Sec.~6; Theorem~5, proof.)
\end{enumerate}
