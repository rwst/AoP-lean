\paragraph{Setting.} Theorem~5 says that the Hilbert-space formulation QSTO:H is inconsistent
with three physical conditions. The brief proves four mathematical propositions (Sec.~2) and
then argues each part of Theorem~5 from one of them (Sec.~4). The propositions are checked in
Lean, and Theorem~5 is stated and proved as \lean{theorem5}, with each condition in its
minimum form (see the open problems, ``Precise readings''). Of the additional remarks of
Sec.~5, the one on the domain of the Schr\"odinger equation is checked too, for the harmonic
oscillator in the number basis and for the free particle in position space, and so is the one
on commutator relations, for position and momentum on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ (see
``Differences'').

\paragraph{Differences from the brief.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Proposition~1.} Both parts are stated for linear maps, without Mathlib's partially
  defined operators. Discontinuity is proved for an unbounded linear map on any normed
  space, which includes $\mathcal{D}(A)$ with the norm inherited from $\mathcal{H}$.
  Hellinger--Toeplitz is proved for an everywhere-defined symmetric map; self-adjointness is
  not needed.
\item \emph{Proposition~2.} The number operator \lean{numberOp} is the diagonal operator
  with entries $n$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, on its maximal domain, as a partially defined
  operator (\lean{LinearPMap}); its domain is \lean{numberDomain}
  $= \{x : \sum_n n^2|x_n|^2 < \infty\}$. Self-adjointness is proved for every diagonal
  operator with real entries (\lean{lp.diagonal}, in \lean{ForMathlib}). The expectation
  \lean{expN}~$x = \sum_n n\,|x_n|^2 / \|x\|^2$ is defined on all of $\ell^2$ and equals
  $\operatorname{Re}\braket{x|Nx}/\|x\|^2$ on $\mathcal{D}(N)$ (\lean{expN_eq_inner}).
  Discontinuity is proved within $\mathcal{D}(N)\setminus\{0\}$ with the subspace topology,
  as in the minimum form of [B]COBS. The sequence $x_j$ is the brief's.
\item \emph{Proposition~3.} The wave functions are $\psi = (e^{-x^2}/\sqrt\pi)^{1/2}$
  (\lean{gaussState}) and $\phi = (1/(\pi(1+y^2)))^{1/2}$, as elements of
  $L^2(\mathbb{R})$. Both frames use the same space $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ with Lebesgue measure.
  The position operators are both multiplication by the coordinate, \lean{positionOp}, on
  their common maximal domain \lean{positionDomain} $= \{\psi : x\psi \in L^2\}$; their
  self-adjointness is not formalized. That $f$ is strictly increasing and onto
  $\mathbb{R}$, and that $g = f^{-1}$ has $g' > 0$, are proved; smoothness is not, only
  differentiability, which is all the unitary needs. The unitary $U$ is
  \lean{Lp.compDiffeo}, proved unitary for any bijection $g$ of $\mathbb{R}$ that is
  differentiable with a differentiable inverse, with weight $\sqrt{|g'|}$; its inverse $V$ is
  the same construction for $g^{-1}$. The operator $UXU^{-1}$ and its domain are not
  formalized. Mathlib has no error function; \lean{Real.erf} is defined in
  \lean{ForMathlib}.
\item \emph{Proposition~4.} The classification of separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
  is new in \lean{ForMathlib}. The positive integers $n, m$ appear as $n + 1, m + 1$.
\item \emph{Theorem~5.} Stated as \lean{theorem5}, a conjunction of three parts, one per
  condition, each for one particular system: the oscillator (Proposition~2), one spatial
  degree of freedom (Proposition~3), and any two numbers $n+1$, $m+1$ of continuous degrees
  of freedom (Proposition~4). ``Inconsistent'' is read as the failure of the minimum form of
  the condition for that system. The [B]COBS part states that $N$ is self-adjoint on
  $\mathcal{D}(N)$, that $E_N(x) = \operatorname{Re}\braket{x|Nx}/\|x\|^2$ there, and that
  $E_N$ is not continuous on $\mathcal{D}(N)\setminus\{0\}$. The [B]WDQ part states
  $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(X)$, $U\psi = \phi$ a.e.\ and $U\psi \notin \mathcal{D}(Y)$, for the
  specific unitary $U$ of the change of frame (for an arbitrary unitary the statement would
  be trivial). The [B]STDIST part states that a unitary $L^2(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}) \to
  L^2(\mathbb{R}^{m+1})$ exists and carries the QSTO:H model. For [B]STDIST, that a
  unitary carries rays, density operators, self-adjoint operators, the Born rule and the
  entropy is proved for any unitary between complex Hilbert spaces (\lean{carriesQSTO}).
  Rays are elements of \lean{Projectivization}; density operators and the entropy are those
  of \lean{ForPhyslib} (positive, trace class, trace one; entropy in $[0,\infty]$);
  self-adjoint operators are partially defined (\lean{LinearPMap}). The closing remarks on
  classical cutoffs and on Schwartz spaces are not formalized.
\item \emph{Sec.~5, the domain of the Schr\"odinger equation.} The brief says that the
  equation is valid only on $\mathcal{D}(H)$. This is read dynamically: the evolution
  $e^{-itH}$ is defined on all of $\mathcal{H}$, but its orbit $t \mapsto e^{-itH}\psi$
  satisfies $i\,\tfrac{d}{dt}\psi(t) = H\psi(t)$ exactly when $\psi \in \mathcal{D}(H)$, and is
  differentiable at no time otherwise. It is proved for the harmonic oscillator in the number
  basis, $H = N$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$, in units with $\hbar\omega = 1$ and without the
  zero-point energy, which only adds a global phase. The general statement is proved for every
  real diagonal operator on $\ell^2$ (\lean{lp.diagonalEvolution}, in \lean{ForMathlib}), and
  for every real multiplication operator on $L^2(\mu)$ (\lean{L2.mulEvolution}). In position
  space, the momentum operator is $P = \mathcal{F}^{-1}(2\pi\xi)\mathcal{F}$ with Mathlib's
  unitary Fourier transform on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, whose convention
  $\hat\psi(\xi) = \int e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\psi(x)\,dx$ gives the factor $2\pi$ ($\hbar = 1$).
  Its maximal domain is identified with $H^1(\mathbb{R})$ in Mathlib's definition
  (\lean{MemSobolev}~1~2, through the Bessel potential); that this is the space of $L^2$
  functions whose distributional derivative is in $L^2$ is not proved here. The state outside
  $\mathcal{D}(P)$ is $\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi$, for the Cauchy packet $\phi$ of
  Proposition~3. The Schr\"odinger equation is checked in position space for the free
  particle, $H_0 = \mathcal{F}^{-1}(2\pi^2\xi^2)\mathcal{F}$ (mass~1); that $H_0 = P^2/2$ as
  partially defined operators is not proved. For a potential $V \ne 0$, see the open
  problems. For the potential part, $V(y) = y^2$ and the state $\phi = U\psi$ of Proposition~3 are used:
  $\phi \in L^2$, but $V\phi \notin L^2$; for $V(y) = y$ this is
  \lean{frameUnitary_gaussState_not_mem}.
\item \emph{Sec.~5, commutator relations.} The brief says that $[X,P]/(i\hbar) = I$ cannot
  hold on the whole of $\mathcal{H}$, since the left side is unbounded, and must be restricted
  to a domain. Both halves are proved, with $\hbar = 1$. The commutator \lean{commutatorXP} is
  $XP - PX$ on its natural domain $\{\psi \in \mathcal{D}(X) \cap \mathcal{D}(P) : P\psi \in
  \mathcal{D}(X),\ X\psi \in \mathcal{D}(P)\}$. Mathlib composes partially defined operators
  only when the range of one lies in the domain of the other, so the composition on the
  natural domain is new in \lean{ForMathlib} (\lean{LinearPMap.compMax}). That the relation
  cannot hold everywhere is proved in two forms: $\mathcal{D}([X,P]) \ne L^2(\mathbb{R})$;
  and, on any nonzero Hilbert space, no everywhere-defined symmetric $X$, $P$ satisfy
  $XP - PX = i\hbar I$. The second form is the Wielandt--Wintner theorem (in a nontrivial
  normed algebra no commutator is a nonzero scalar, \lean{commutator_ne_algebraMap}, in
  \lean{ForMathlib}) combined with Hellinger--Toeplitz. That the restricted relation holds is
  proved on all of $\mathcal{D}([X,P])$: in the tempered distributions, $X$ is
  multiplication by $x$ and $P = -i\,d/dx$, the Leibniz rule
  $\tfrac{d}{dx}(xu) = u + x\tfrac{du}{dx}$ holds
  (\lean{TemperedDistribution.lineDerivOp_smulLeftCLM}, in \lean{ForMathlib}), and the
  embedding $L^2 \to \mathcal{S}'$ is injective. Schwartz functions lie in
  $\mathcal{D}([X,P])$, so it is dense. Closures and self-adjoint extensions are not
  considered.
\item The other additional remark of Section~5, on point-wise values, is not formalized.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Problems found in the brief.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cross-references lack their label: the proof of Theorem~5 has ``This satisfies the
  conditions for 2'', ``As shown in 3'' and ``By 4'' for Propositions~2, 3 and~4.
\item ``Schwartz spaces present the same problem'' is asserted without proof, and without
  saying which structure on $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is meant (see the open problems).
\item QSTO:H still allows any separable Hilbert space, finite-dimensional ones included. This
  no longer affects Theorem~5, whose proof reads the inconsistency as the failure of a
  condition for one particular system, but the brief could say so.
\end{itemize}
