\noindent
Open problems raised by the technical brief in this directory, and by its Lean formalization
(see \lean{formalization-log.tex}).
Items marked \textsc{[blocks verification]} stand in the way of checking a main
claim of the brief as the brief states it: a \emph{formalization gap} can be closed
in Lean alone, while a \emph{brief issue} needs the brief to be corrected or made
precise first.

No item below blocks the verification of a main claim of the brief.

\section*{From the brief}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The quantum case.} The conclusion says that ``in general, the uncertainty
  principle is a consequence of the entropic lower bound'', since pure quantum states have zero
  entropy. The classical bound $\hbar/e \approx 0.37\,\hbar$ is weaker than Heisenberg's
  $\hbar/2$, and no entropic derivation of the quantum bound is given. Whether, and in what form,
  the quantum principle follows from the zero entropy of pure states is open. (Brief, Sec.~3.)
\item \textbf{The equality case of eq.~(5).} The brief says when equality holds in eq.~(3) (see
  the log, ``Problems found'', first item) but not in eq.~(5). Equality in Gibbs' inequality
  would require $\rho = |\{f, g\}|\,(G \circ (f, g))$ almost everywhere, with $G$ a product of
  Gaussians, and then also that $(f, g)$ is onto up to a null set. The exact conditions are not
  stated. (Brief, eq.~(5).)
\end{enumerate}

\section*{From the formalization}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Several degrees of freedom.} ZENT and CUP are formalized for one DOF, on a density
  on $\mathbb R^2$. That the marginal of a density on $(\mathbb R^2)^n$ is again an ensemble
  with finite entropy and finite second moments is not formalized. (Log, ``Differences'', second
  item.)
\item \textbf{Entropy in new variables.} The brief's identity
  $S[\rho_{fg}] = S[\rho_{qp}] + E[\ln|\{f, g\}|]$ for the density $\rho_{fg}$ of $(f, g)$ is
  not formalized. Eq.~(5) is proved without forming $\rho_{fg}$. (Log, ``Differences'', fourth
  item.)
\item \textbf{The Lagrange computation.} The variational computation of the proof of Theorem~2
  is not formalized; Gibbs' inequality replaces it. (Log, ``Differences'', first item.)
\end{enumerate}
