\noindent
Open problems raised by the technical brief in this directory, and by its Lean formalization
(see \lean{formalization-log.tex}).
Items marked \textsc{[blocks verification]} stand in the way of checking a main
claim of the brief as the brief states it: a \emph{formalization gap} can be closed
in Lean alone, while a \emph{brief issue} needs the brief to be corrected or made
precise first.

\section*{From the brief}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Proposition~6 for infinite entropy is a proof sketch.} For
  $S[\rho_{qp}] = -\infty$ the brief takes densities $\eta_j$ with finite logarithmic
  expectations tending to $+\infty$ and transports them to a uniform density on a bounded
  rectangle, claiming the variances stay bounded while $E[\ln J_{T_j} \mid \rho_{qp}] \to
  +\infty$; for $S[\rho_{qp}] = +\infty$, that every admissible frame keeps an infinite entropy
  and so an infinite variance. Neither step is argued. The finite-entropy case, on which
  Theorem~12 rests, is proved, with the steps the sketch leaves open filled in: the
  approximation needs a smoothing scale that depends on position, since a finite entropy gives
  no moment of $\rho_{qp}$, and the finite variances follow from Young's inequality
  $ab \le a\ln a - a + e^b$ (Log, ``Differences from the brief''). (Brief, Sec.~2,
  Proposition~6.)
\item \textbf{The quantum case in the conclusion.} The conclusion places the lower entropic bound
  in quantum mechanics: pure states have zero entropy and mixed states non-negative entropy. The
  von Neumann entropy vanishes on every pure state whatever its $\sigma_q\sigma_p$, so this bound
  constrains no uncertainty product, and the classical argument turns $S \ge 0$ into
  $\hbar/e$, not $\hbar/2$. Which entropic bound plays the role of [B]ZENT in quantum mechanics is
  open in the brief; two candidates, both strictly stronger than zero entropy, are formalized as
  hypotheses (next section, last item). Not a main claim. (Brief, Sec.~6; Log, ``Problems
  found in the brief''.)
\end{enumerate}

\section*{From the formalization}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Extended variance and infinite entropy.} Variances are Bochner integrals and
  entropies real numbers, so the brief's extended variance
  $\inf_a E[(X - a)^2] \in [0, \infty]$ and well-defined infinite entropies are not modelled.
  GCUR and CUR are stated for coordinates with finite variances, where the brief also counts
  the automatic case of an infinite variance. Open: the infinite-entropy part of
  Proposition~6 ($\inf_T U_\rho(T) = \mp\infty$), and the remarks ``Infinite and undefined
  entropy'' (the generalized inequalities cannot guarantee that the entropy exists; admitting
  every density with extended entropy breaks convexity) and ``Entropy and variance'' (finite
  entropy without finite canonical variances).
\item \textbf{Definition~2 in full.} \lean{IsAdmissible} asks for $C^1$ coordinates, injectivity
  and a nowhere vanishing Jacobian. That the range is open and the inverse $C^1$ follows from the
  inverse function theorem, but is not formalized, since nothing uses it. The remark ``Bounded
  coordinate ranges'' is not formalized either.
\item \textbf{Convexity in KL form.} The remark on convexity is formalized as its two bounds
  (\lean{entropy_mix_bounds}); the identity
  $S[\rho_\lambda] - \lambda S[\rho] - (1 - \lambda)S[\sigma] = \lambda D(\rho\,\|\,\rho_\lambda)
  + (1 - \lambda)D(\sigma\,\|\,\rho_\lambda)$ in between is not.
\item \textbf{Several degrees of freedom.} For $(\mathbb R^2)^{n+1}$, [B]ZENT and the inequality
  of CUR are stated for the marginals of the coordinate pairs $(q_i, p_i)$, and Theorem~9 is
  proved for CUR there. GCUR for the marginals, and independent DOFs in canonical coordinates
  that mix the pairs, are not considered.
\item \textbf{Quantum entropic bounds.} The bounds of Bia\l{}ynicki-Birula and Mycielski and of
  Lieb on the Wehrl entropy, from which Heisenberg's relation is derived with the brief's
  Gaussian bound (\lean{heisenberg_of_entropicUncertainty}, \lean{heisenberg_of_wehrlBound}),
  are hypotheses. Proving them needs Beckner's sharp Hausdorff--Young inequality, and the
  Fourier transform and Husimi function of a wave function, none of which are formed here.
\end{enumerate}
