\noindent
Open problems raised by the technical brief in this directory, and by its Lean formalization
(see \lean{formalization-log.tex}).
Each item is marked \emph{Formalization gap}, when the mathematics is known and only its
formalization is missing, or \emph{Research}, when the question itself is open.

\section*{From the brief}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Time-dependent evolution.} \emph{Research} in infinite dimension; in finite
  dimension a brief issue, answered there. The equivalence of DR-SCEQ, DR-UNIT, DR-EV, DR-PROB and
  DR-INFO without EVTI, for two-parameter families $\Phi_{t_0 \to t_1}$ and a time-dependent
  Hamiltonian $H(t)$. The brief only says ``We are going to restrict the dynamics to the
  time-independent case''. That case is formalized as the brief writes it, with the two-parameter
  [B]EVMIX, EVTI, and DR-SCEQ with $H(t)$ (\lean{TwoParameter.schrodinger_tfae}). Without EVTI
  there is no one-parameter group, and DR-UNIT, DR-EV, DR-PROB and DR-INFO have to be restated
  for the propagators $\Phi_{t_0 \to t_1}$, which the brief does not do.
  \emph{Answer in finite dimension} (\lean{further-results.tex}, Sec.~1). Restated for every
  $\Phi_{t_0\to t_1}$, with DR-UNIT as conjugation by $U(t_1)U(t_0)^\dagger$ for a family of
  unitaries $U(t)$, the four conditions are equivalent
  (\lean{SchroedingerEvolution.FurtherResults.schrodinger_tfae}), and DR-SCEQ with $H(t)$ is
  equivalent to each of them together with EVDIFF ($t \mapsto \Phi_{r\to t}(\rho)$ is
  differentiable at every $t > r$) (\lean{SchroedingerEvolution.FurtherResults.drSceq_tfae}).
  The step DR-INFO + EVDIFF $\Rightarrow$ DR-SCEQ is from the brief ``Schr\"odinger equation is
  equivalent to information conservation''
  (\lean{UnitaryEntropy.TwoParameter.drSceq_iff_drInfo_and_evDiff}). Unlike under EVTI, EVDIFF
  cannot be dropped: a continuous family $U(t)$ need not be differentiable (not formalized).
  \emph{Open in infinite dimension}. With unbounded $H(t)$ the propagators of DR-SCEQ are known to
  exist only under conditions on the domains of the $H(t)$ (Kato, J.\ Math.\ Soc.\ Japan 5
  (1953) 208), and recovering $H(t)$ from a unitary propagator needs domain conditions that the
  brief's setting does not supply. (Brief, Sec.~3, before Condition EVTI, and Condition DR-SCEQ;
  log, ``The conditions as the brief writes them''.)
\item \textbf{Isolation as determinism and reversibility.} \emph{Research}, once isolation
  is defined independently; as the brief stands, a brief issue. ``Conceptually, all these are equivalent to saying
  that the system is isolated, as future and past states are determined, and only determined, by
  the present state of the system.'' Isolation is not defined, so this equivalence is not stated
  as a theorem. If isolation means what the sentence says, determinism and reversibility, as in
  the book \emph{Assumptions of Physics} (chapter on classical mechanics, ``isolated, i.e.\
  deterministic and reversible''), then it amounts to every $\Phi_t$ being a bijection of the
  density operators, and the claim is Proposition~1 together with Theorem~3, both formalized
  (\lean{affine_pure_state_correspondence}, \lean{schrodinger_tfae}). A theorem with content
  needs a definition of isolation that does not refer to the evolution itself, for example
  through a composite system whose reduced dynamics does not depend on the state of the
  environment; whether that is equivalent to unitary evolution is a research question.
  (Brief, Sec.~6.)
\end{enumerate}

\section*{From the formalization}
\noindent None found. Beyond finite dimension the formalization has a part in any dimension,
which covers unbounded $H$, and the shift semigroup on $L^2(0,\infty)$, which shows that the
reconstructed past clauses of DR-PROB and DR-INFO are needed there
(\lean{shift_semigroup_counterexample}; log, ``Problems found in the brief'').
